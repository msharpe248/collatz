## Findings that affect proof strategy

**The no-go result is narrower than some prose suggests.** `NoGo.lean:205` excludes a globally bounded positive multiplier `W`, a fixed window `B`, and a fixed contraction factor `ρ < 1`. It does not exclude every finite-state argument, every finite program, or a proof using unbounded input-dependent windows. README phrases such as “no bounded-memory argument can settle” are broader than the formal result. This distinction matters because adaptive arithmetic certificates remain a legitimate research direction, although their completeness is unresolved.

**The critical boundary cannot be replaced by a strict density gap.** `density_floor` is a finite lower bound, tending toward the critical density as the orbit floor increases. It does not prove that every divergent orbit has liminf density strictly greater than `log 2 / log 3`. In `Ideal.lean`, `Supercritical n` actually means boundedness of `idealC`, and the tracking theorems correctly require it. The surrounding assertion that this is automatically the band containing all divergent orbits is not justified by `density_floor`.

Indeed, the correction increments have the shape

`c_(t+1) - c_t = w_t * 2^t / 3^(j_t+1)`.

A uniform asymptotic density gap above critical gives geometric summability; merely approaching the critical lower bound does not. A future proof must explicitly cover both bounded-correction and unbounded-correction cases. Also, the introductory mod-one display in `Ideal.lean` has a minus sign on the tail correction, whereas the proved `fract_ideal` correctly has a plus sign.

**The Sturmian level theorem is a conditional theorem, not yet a family exclusion.** Its precise obligations are `hL` (separation), `hT` (uniform visits), and `hS` (size margin). The paper records this accurately in its scope section. Large partial quotients must occur on the positive-error convergent subsequence used by the theorem; “infinitely many large partial quotients” without that qualification is insufficient for this particular corollary.

**One experimental upper bound omits the affine correction.** The introduction to `analysis/sturmian_prefix_power.py` writes `log₂(n_s) ≤ log₂(n) + j_s log₂3 - s`. As an orbit upper bound this is false: for `n=3, s=1`, the Terras value is `5`, whereas `3·3/2=4.5`. The exact formula has a positive correction. Its `best_margin` statistic omits that correction as well. These plots can suggest growth trends, but should be replaced by a rigorously bounded score before they become evidence for a quantified exclusion. `Sturmian.tail_le_of_itin` already provides an appropriate bound including `3N+3s`.

**The nested repositories have different trust and compatibility boundaries.** For example, the pinned Monks `Proof.lean` declares `primitive_root_two_mod_three_pow` and `back_trace_dense` as axioms. Knight modules also contain mathematical axioms. The rwst corpus deliberately contains cited axioms, including automatic-word and transcendence inputs. Some `sorry` occurrences elsewhere are challenge stubs, so their mere presence does not establish a headline dependency. Audit the exact theorem to be imported with `#print axioms`.

Your main toolchain is `v4.27.0-rc1`; the ten nested roots range from `v4.21.0` through `v4.34.0-rc2`. Use them as pinned reference projects and port narrow lemmas with explicit bridges. Directly importing the entire library into the main project is not currently a drop-in operation.

**Keep the two ways Collatz could fail visible.** Excluding unbounded orbits still leaves nontrivial cycles. The existing cycle results bound or exclude classes of cycles, not all of them. A theorem reducing to universal descent already handles both; a theorem about divergent itineraries does not by itself.

## Focus 1: an unconditional Sturmian case

Use the slope `α = 1/√2 = [0;1,2,2,…]`, for every intercept. This lies above critical and below `3/4`, and it has an elementary quadratic recurrence for its convergents. It offers a narrower first target than developing the entire general continued-fraction and three-distance theory.

Write `η = α log₂3 - 1`. For positive-error convergents, set `q=q_k`, `Q=q_(k+1)`, and `G=q+Q`. The exact logarithmic margin at the worst endpoint `s=G` is

`Q(1-η) - q(1+2η) - 3 - log₂3 - log₂(3N+3(q+Q)+1)`.

Here `Q ≥ 2q` and `η < 1/4`, so the linear part is at least `q(1-4η)`, with a fixed positive coefficient. Along this quadratic recurrence, `G=O(q)`, and the logarithmic term is dominated. This supplies the size condition once the rotation inputs are established.

First Lean step: an endpoint wrapper around `sturmian_level`, reducing the family `hS` to its endpoint at `G`. Next prove the specific convergent recurrence and signs, separation `hL`, and the exact half-open visit statement `hT` for every intercept. Then package the unbounded sequence of levels to exclude every natural-number realization at this slope.

The main risk is `hT`: a general reference to the three-distance theorem is insufficient unless its precise mesh and endpoint conventions imply the required visits. The Hercher `Collatz/CFBestApprox.lean` is a useful reference for convergent signs and recurrences; its toolchain differs, so audit and port only what is needed.

Child directions: other quadratic slopes with a positive margin; a negative-error version of the level theorem; longer return gaps for golden-tail slopes. Each is a theorem about a specified family, not a coverage theorem for all divergent itineraries.

## Focus 2: controlled swaps instead of literal repetition ★

For a finite parity word `w` of length `L` and odd count `o`, let its affine correction be `d(w)`, so its branch composition is `(3^o x + d(w))/2^L`. The two blocks `01` and `10` have the same multiplier and corrections `2` and `1`, respectively. More generally, replacing `u01v` by `u10v` gives the exact identity

`d(u01v) - d(u10v) = 2^|u| * 3^(ones(v))`.

For equal-length, equal-odd-count words realized from states `x,y`, obtain

`2^L (y' - x') = 3^o (y-x) + d(v)-d(u)`.

This suggests extending the prefix-power machinery to repeated blocks modified by controlled adjacent swaps. Such blocks can share their total growth multiplier while having a correction difference expressible by a small weighted sum. On an unbounded orbit, two distinct times cannot subsequently merge, so the compensated integer is nonzero. Divisibility then yields a contradiction whenever an independent bound makes its absolute value smaller than `2^L`.

First Lean step: define a finite-word affine correction and prove the concatenation and adjacent-swap identities, connecting it to `dcoef`. The repository has the needed cocycle and closed-form ingredients. As an exploratory arithmetic check, I exhaustively verified the swap identity for all adjacent `01` occurrences in binary words of lengths 2–9: 1,793 exact-integer checks passed. This is not a Lean proof or an exclusion theorem.

The main risk is not establishing the identity; it is bounding the weighted defect sum. Few mismatches do not imply high 2-adic agreement: one early mismatch can destroy all initial common parity precision. In particular, a general “r mismatches preserve divisibility by `2^(L-r)`” claim is false.

Child directions: opposite swaps with cancellation; a finite library of equal-weight block replacements; explicit substitution families whose block discrepancies have a recursive bound. Do not assume a generic irregular itinerary admits a useful swap description.

## Focus 3: couple survivor cylinders to ordinary integer height

For a length-`k` word, let `r<2^k` be its parity residue, `j` its odd count, and `b=T^k(r)`. Every nonnegative integer in the cylinder has the form `r+2^k q`, and

`T^k(r+2^k q) = b+3^j q`.

For each earlier prefix `i≤k`, the corresponding coefficient of `q` is `3^(j_i) 2^(k-i)`. Thus both the height condition `1≤r+2^k q≤H` and every prefix survival condition are affine inequalities in the same integer `q`. Their intersection is an exactly checkable integer interval, possibly empty. This makes the interaction of 2-adic input information and 3-adic output information concrete.

First Lean step: prove this transport identity and the interval characterization, including `r=0` and `q=0`. At depth `k=ceil(log₂(H+1))`, each cylinder contains at most one seed of height at most `H`. Experiments can then follow the survivors beyond this threshold and identify a recognizable family for which checkpoint inequalities force eventual descent.

The main risk is circularity. Exhaustion for every finite height is already equivalent to universal descent. An infinite compatible parity path defines a 2-adic integer, not necessarily a positive ordinary integer. The least representatives are nondecreasing and represent a natural number exactly when they eventually stabilize; that elementary observation does not prove surviving paths fail to stabilize.

Child directions: threshold-relative survival depths; short sets of incompatible checkpoint inequalities; a provable bound for one independently defined family of survivor plateaus. Require a separate statement for any claimed exhaustive classification.

## Directions to retain, but not mistake for the last step

Generalizing `Rung1.lean` into a reusable integer Mahler/Padé certificate theorem is a good supporting project. Extract functional-equation transport, exact nonvanishing, and height domination as separate certificate fields; replay the existing example before adding substitutions. Aperiodic automatic words are a much larger class than the single excluded substitution, and eliminating even all automatic words would leave unrestricted itineraries.

Continue KL exponent work if the objective is stronger density theorems. It does not supply a route from “many integers converge” to “every integer converges” by itself. Even bounds with exponents approaching one leave room for exceptional integers; constants and uniformity matter too. Likewise, [Tao's theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03562) is an almost-all result in logarithmic density, not a pointwise escape theorem for an arbitrary exceptional orbit.

Monks sufficient sets are attractive only with their full hypotheses and audited dependencies. An arithmetic progression being sufficient means orbit merging coverage, not that every orbit visits that progression. Decreasing intersections of sufficient sets need not remain sufficient without a new argument.
