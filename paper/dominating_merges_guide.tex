\guideheading{What is now verified}
Lean checks 360 arithmetic rules. Each gives a smaller positive seed
that meets the original orbit and inherits full noncontraction if the
original has it. Every rule applies to all nonnegative quotients, so it
covers an infinite progression, not just a tested example.

\guideheading{What noncontraction means}
After $t$ shortcut steps with $j$ odd steps, the multiplicative coefficient
is $3^j/2^t$. A seed is noncontracting when that coefficient is at least
one for every prefix. No infinite noncontracting seed has been constructed.
The aim is to rule out a least positive noncontracting seed by finding
a smaller one whenever the original has that property.

\guideheading{Why the transfer works}
Before the meeting, the replacement's coefficient must stay at least one.
At the meeting, it must be at least as large as the original path's
coefficient. Afterward the paths follow the same values and gain the same
further factors. Those two checks preserve full noncontraction. Shifting the original seed does not automatically preserve
noncontraction; the coefficient comparison is essential.

\guideheading{An example that works for arbitrarily large seeds}
For any whole number $Q\geq0$, take
\[
N=111+4374Q,\qquad M=103+4096Q.
\]
The larger seed reaches $167+6561Q$ in one step; the smaller reaches it
in twelve steps with eight odd steps. The difference $N-M=8+278Q$ stays
positive. Thus a least positive noncontracting seed cannot belong to this
progression. This does not, by itself, prove that every member converges.

\guideheading{How a finite certificate gives an infinite rule}
Adding a multiple of $2^t$ to a seed preserves its first $t$ parities.
The endpoint changes by a corresponding multiple of $3^j$. The rule
chooses increments for the two seeds so these endpoint changes agree,
while the replacement's increment is no larger. Parity validity, merging,
and strict seed ordering then persist for every nonnegative quotient.

The kernel checks the actual endpoints, prefix inequalities, coefficient
comparison, and balanced increments for every listed rule. A general
Lean theorem handles every quotient. Python generates the data; it does
not supply a trusted proof.

\guideheading{What remains open}
The 3,768 successful dominating certificates from the depth-18 search
reduce to 360 distinct progressions. Their validity is kernel certified;
the search's completeness is not. The earlier bounded search still leaves 2,336
seeds unresolved, including 27 and 703; they are not counterexamples.

Most importantly, no theorem says that a suitable rule exists for every
hypothetical least noncontracting seed. Proving that coverage, potentially
with lengths depending on the seed, remains the central gap. Nontrivial
cycles are also not excluded here.

\guideheading{Reproduce}
Run \texttt{python3 analysis/merge\_progressions.py --write-lean} from the
root and \texttt{lake build Collatz.MergeRuleTable} from \texttt{lean/}.
The saved JSON, generated Lean table, and tests make the rules reviewable.
The Collatz conjecture remains unproved.
